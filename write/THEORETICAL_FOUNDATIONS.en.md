# Knowledge Building, Knowledge Forum, and HAKCC

[System guide](SYSTEM_OVERVIEW.en.md) · [Home](../README.md) · [中文](THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS.md)

## Knowledge Building

Marlene Scardamalia and Carl Bereiter's **Knowledge Building (KB)** concerns a community's continuing advancement of public knowledge. Students help frame problems, develop explanations, examine evidence, and choose further inquiry. Individual learning and community knowledge advancement are related, but are not interchangeable outcomes. [Scardamalia & Bereiter (2006)](https://ikit.org/fulltext/2006_KBTheory.pdf).

**Collective cognitive responsibility** asks members to share responsibility for consequential knowledge work: deciding what matters, what remains inadequately explained, and how the community can proceed. HAKCC uses this lens to keep student judgment central to AI collaboration. [Scardamalia (2002)](https://ikit.org/fulltext/inpressCollectiveCog.pdf).

## Knowledge Forum as a design precedent

**Knowledge Forum (KF)** supports public ideas through Notes and Views, connected contributions through Build-on, discourse scaffolding, and higher-order synthesis through Rise-above. It provides an established precedent for making developing knowledge available for continued community improvement. [Scardamalia (2003)](https://ikit.org/fulltext/2003_KFAdvances.htm).

HAKCC retains this domain language and adds context-bound AI conversations, selective uptake with provenance, configurable feedback, and source-visible discussion rooms. These are HAKCC design interpretations. The foundational texts do not provide empirical validation of HAKCC's generative-AI mechanisms.

## Principles interpreted in the platform

The following is a design mapping using the twelve KB principle names discussed in [Tarchi et al. (2013)](https://ikit.org/knowledgebuilding/collaboratory/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Knowledge-building-and-knowledge-forum-getting-started-with-pedagogy-and-technology.pdf). It is not a claim that each principle has been fully realized or validated.

| KB principle | HAKCC interpretation | Work to examine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real ideas, authentic problems | Shared inquiry questions and student Notes | Problems students themselves seek to understand |
| Improvable ideas | Revision, Build-on, and feedback | Substantive changes to an explanation |
| Idea diversity | Multiple Notes, Views, and relation types | Comparison of competing explanations |
| Rise above | Source-visible discussion and synthesis relations | New explanations that address fragmentation or disagreement |
| Epistemic agency | Student choices of questions, AI roles, excerpts, and uptake reasons | Reasons for accepting, rejecting, or revising suggestions |
| Community knowledge, collective responsibility | Shared workspace and contribution links | Members advancing one another's inquiry |
| Democratizing knowledge | Multi-member participation and participation analysis | Opportunities for broader contribution |
| Symmetric knowledge advancement | Connections among ideas and groups | Discoveries informing another inquiry strand |
| Pervasive knowledge building | Persistent course spaces and materials | Questions continued across sessions |
| Constructive uses of authoritative sources | Materials, evidence relations, and retrieval | Sources used to test or revise explanations |
| Knowledge building discourse | Typed Build-on and collaborative discussion | Discourse producing further questions and explanations |
| Concurrent, embedded, transformative assessment | Feedback, revision, and process analysis | Assessment informing further knowledge work |

## AI and student responsibility

HAKCC treats AI suggestions as resources students can question, test, and reconfigure. An adoption reason makes selected uptake an inspectable decision. Retained provenance gives teachers and peers context for that decision. Source Notes remain visible in Rise-above discussions, while participants supply the published synthesis.

Whether these mechanisms support epistemic agency depends on actual interaction, revisions, reasons, and discourse. That question requires empirical research. Merely adding Notes, relations, or AI to a platform does not ensure high-quality knowledge building.
